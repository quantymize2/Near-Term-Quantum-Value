# The Quantum Capacity Is Already There

![Quantum Optimization Insights: Multiplexing](./multiplexing-analogy-header.png)

*Part of the Quantum Optimization Insights series — QuantyMize*

---

The problem looked like a supply problem.

It was a scheduling problem.

Early telephone networks scaled the obvious way: one physical circuit per conversation. By the 1920s, carriers were staring down a capacity ceiling that no amount of new cable could solve fast enough.

The fix wasn't more wire.

It was multiplexing — splitting a single physical line into many logical channels by interleaving signals in time or frequency.

The line's raw capacity didn't change.

What changed was how intelligently that capacity got allocated.

But look closer at where the value is actually getting lost, and a familiar pattern shows up.

The physical system usually has more room than the operating picture suggests — the binding constraint is how the existing capacity gets scheduled and allocated, not the raw infrastructure itself.

A grid operator's congestion, a logistics network's "full" warehouses, a spectrum's "exhausted" bandwidth — these often trace back to an allocation problem layered on top of infrastructure that already has latent room.

This is the same reframe multiplexing made a century ago: the ceiling isn't physical, it's computational. Better allocation of the decisions layered on top of existing infrastructure can unlock capacity that's already there.

Multiplexing didn't add capacity to the wire. It found the capacity that was already there, waiting on a smarter map of how to use it.
