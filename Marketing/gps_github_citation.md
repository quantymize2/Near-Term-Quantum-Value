# GPS Analogy — GitHub Citation Version
*(citation-only; not cross-linked from LinkedIn or Substack)*

**Technical summary:** GPS satellites transmit at approximately 50 watts of power; the signal received at Earth's surface is many orders of magnitude weaker due to the roughly 20,000 km transmission distance. GPS positioning accuracy depends on precise time synchronization between satellite and ground-based receiver clocks rather than on signal strength. Combined relativistic effects — special relativity from satellite orbital velocity (~14,000 km/h, causing satellite clocks to run slower by approximately 7 microseconds/day relative to ground) and general relativity from reduced gravitational potential at orbital altitude (causing satellite clocks to run faster by approximately 45 microseconds/day) — produce a net drift of approximately 38 microseconds/day between satellite and ground clocks if uncorrected, sufficient to introduce positioning errors of several kilometers per day. GPS engineers correct for this by adjusting satellite atomic clock frequencies prior to launch so that the combined relativistic effects result in synchronization with ground time once in orbit.

**Applied argument:** A system's practical accuracy can depend on correcting a subtle, non-obvious structural mismatch rather than on increasing raw signal or resource strength. This is directly analogous to the argument that quantum optimization solution quality depends more on correct problem-to-hardware mapping than on raw qubit count — a mismapped problem is not fixed by adding more qubits, any more than GPS accuracy would have been fixed by a stronger transmitter.
