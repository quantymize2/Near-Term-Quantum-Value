# Quantum Optimization's Precedent: The 1930s Refinery Breakthrough

**Published:** July 2026 · QuantyMize

**Summary:** Historical parallel between catalytic cracking's transformation of oil refining and how quantum optimization applies the same reframing principle to grid, telecom, and logistics networks.

## Historical reference
- Catalytic cracking (commercialized in the late 1930s, e.g. the Houdry process) substantially increased gasoline yield from a barrel of crude oil using existing refinery infrastructure.
- The gain came from a process and decision-making innovation — which hydrocarbon chains to break apart and reassemble — not from new oil wells or newly built refineries.

## Framing
Physical infrastructure sets a ceiling on possible output. Decision-making systems determine how close operations get to that ceiling. Constraints assumed to be physical are often decision or process constraints in disguise.

## Connection to quantum optimization
This is the same principle QuantyMize applies to quantum optimization: gains come from reframing a decision or allocation problem, not from adding more qubits or more hardware.
