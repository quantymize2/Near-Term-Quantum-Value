# Why Insert #3 — GitHub Citation Version
*(citation-only; not cross-linked from LinkedIn or Substack)*

**Observation:** Organizations frequently develop sophisticated internal narratives to explain underperformance (e.g., attributing shortfalls to market conditions, timing, or one-off events) rather than investigating root causes. Over time, the skill of articulating a plausible explanation for a loss can become more refined than the organization's actual capability to prevent that loss from recurring — a documented pattern in organizational behavior research on excuse-making and attribution bias in performance reviews.

**Applied argument:** A well-told explanation for underperformance does not change the underlying outcome; it changes how much scrutiny the outcome receives. This is directly relevant to evaluating optimization and decision-support tools: a smooth explanation for a suboptimal result is not evidence the result was actually the best available outcome.
