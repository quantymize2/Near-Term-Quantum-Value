# Panama Canal Analogy — GitHub Citation Version
*(citation-only; not cross-linked from LinkedIn or Substack)*

**Historical reference:** The French Panama Canal Company, led by Ferdinand de Lesseps (fresh off the 1869 completion of the Suez Canal), began construction of a sea-level canal across the Panama isthmus in 1881, replicating the flat-cut design used at Suez. The attempt failed due to unanticipated mountainous terrain, unstable geology causing repeated landslides, and tropical disease (yellow fever and malaria) that killed an estimated 22,000 workers; the company declared bankruptcy in 1889. The United States acquired the project in 1904 and, under chief engineer John Stevens, redesigned the canal around a lock system — a series of gravity-fed water chambers that raise and lower vessels over the elevation change rather than cutting through it at sea level. The canal opened in 1914.

**Applied argument:** A design that succeeds under one set of physical constraints (flat terrain) can fail completely under different constraints (mountainous terrain) if applied without modification. The historically effective fix was not increased effort or resources but a structurally different approach to the same underlying obstacle — directly analogous to the argument that mismapped quantum optimization problems are not solved by additional qubits, but by re-structuring how the problem is represented.
