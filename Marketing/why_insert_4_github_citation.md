# Why Insert #4 — GitHub Citation Version
*(citation-only; not cross-linked from LinkedIn or Substack)*

**Observation:** Organizational performance standards tend to erode gradually rather than through a single deliberate decision — a documented pattern sometimes described as "standard drift," where a benchmark quietly shifts from an aspirational target ("is this the best possible outcome") to a satisficing threshold ("is this an acceptable outcome"). Once a satisficing threshold becomes the default, it tends to remain uncontested, since meeting it removes the immediate pressure to revisit it.

**Applied argument:** Accepting "good enough" as the working standard for a decision-support or optimization outcome is functionally different from accepting the best available outcome — the two are frequently conflated in practice, particularly when the tool producing the result is a black box and the gap between "good enough" and "best possible" is not directly visible to the decision-maker.
